# Challenges resolve through a Showdown where the claimant picks the card

A Challenge no longer resolves on the server the moment it is made. It opens a Showdown phase (`showdown`): the challenged Player has 20 seconds to reveal one of their own Hidden Cards, and every Player watches it happen. The revealed card decides the result. If it is the claimed Role, the Challenge fails: that card is shuffled into the Deck, a replacement is dealt into the same hand position, and the challenger then loses Influence. Any other card makes the Challenge succeed, and that card becomes the challenged Player's lost Influence in the same step. A Player who holds the claimed Role may still reveal the other card on purpose; that counts as a successful Challenge, and the client asks the Player to confirm first. On timeout, the claimed Role is revealed if held, otherwise a random card.

This replaces the old engine behavior, where the engine checked the hand and proved the claim automatically. The tabletop rule (ADR 0007) is that the challenged player shows the card themselves, and declining to show it is the same as not having it, so this design is closer to the physical game, not a variant. It also turns the most dramatic moment of a turn into something every Player can see: who challenged whom over which Role, and the reveal itself. The hidden-information boundary from ADR 0002 still holds. Spectators get only the card count, the position the claimant is pointing at, and the revealed card's Role and position (`challengeResolved.role` / `slot`). The pointer position is relayed by the server outside the Game state and is rate-limited.

Choosing which card to lose (Coup, Assassination, failed Challenge) also has a 20-second timer with a random Default Choice. Its enlarged picker is private to that Player; everyone else sees a waiting line. After a Showdown the server extends the next timer by the length of the reveal animation, so the animation does not use up the next player's countdown.

**Considered Options**: Keeping the automatic proof was rejected because it hides the decision the tabletop game gives the challenged player and leaves no moment to watch. A separate "prove" step followed by a "choose loss" step was rejected because it asks the challenged player to choose twice when one revealed card already decides both.
