# Background Music as pre-rendered stems mixed by state

Background Music is one composed loop, rendered offline from a score with sampled instruments into stems that all loop in sync in the browser. A game state only changes each stem's gain, starting on the next beat, so the music changes instrumentation without ever changing tune or losing its place. The score, the renderer and the per-state gain table live in the separate `game-music-studio` repository. Its `render.py game` step exports six MP3s and a `manifest.json` (states, gains, priority, beat and loop timing) into `packages/client/src/music/`. The client reads that manifest and never keeps a gain table of its own. Which state holds is derived in the client from the Session (`musicStateFor`): the server knows nothing about music.

The stems ship premixed per band (piano, sax and bass in one file per instrument set). The Muse Sounds EULA allows its sounds only mixed into your own recording and forbids distributing them as sounds or samples. Every state plays a band's three stems at one gain anyway, and premixing also cuts the download. Each file carries half a second of the loop's own tail before it and head after it, and the client loops the window between them, so no browser's MP3 decoder delay can open a gap at the seam.

The winner's victory loop is the one separate piece. It plays only once the game is over, so it never interrupts play. It is the leitmotif in D major on its own 16 bars at the main tempo (48 s), shipped as one more file with its loop length in the manifest's `loops` and its gain in the same table. It does not loop from the shared origin, and neither entering nor leaving it waits longer than the next beat. A win starts it from bar 1, the crash, at full level on that beat, while every other track falls over the one-second rise time, so the minor music does not linger under the major. Leaving it also starts on the next beat, so a rematch is heard at once. On that beat the main loop restarts from bar 1, which opens on the sax's held A, a note D major and D minor share. It rises over a second while the victory loop falls over two, the usual rise and fall times. `render.py` sets its loudness from the in-play mix (0.5 LU above it, BS.1770), so winning out of a coup or out of a showdown both land without a jump.

**Considered Options**: Synthesising the music live with Web Audio (the prototype on `prototype/background-music`) was rejected: it costs no download, but it sounded like a demo next to sampled instruments. Separate pieces per state were rejected because switching tunes mid-game is jarring, whereas a layered loop can change on any beat. Ogg Vorbis was rejected because Safari, and so every iOS browser and WeChat, decodes it in Web Audio only from 18.4.

**Consequences**: Changing the music, a gain or a state means re-running `render.py` and copying `game/` over `src/music/`. A new state needs its condition added to `musicStateFor`, whose `Record` over the manifest's states fails to type-check until it is. Playback depends on about 19 MB of audio, loaded only while the music is unmuted; the lobby's band comes first.
